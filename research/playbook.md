# Playbook: Community-Led Growth for B2B SaaS

This playbook translates the research portfolio into practical, opinionated 
guidance. It is not a comprehensive framework. It's what I would actually 
do if asked to build community-led growth at a small B2B SaaS company 
tomorrow.

This file assumes you have read [`sources.md`](sources.md) and 
[`patterns-and-tensions.md`](patterns-and-tensions.md). If you have not, 
start there.

## 6. Who I Would Not Recommend Following

Two of the ten experts I studied are the ones I would not recommend as 
starting points for junior growth marketers learning CLG. This is not 
a judgment on their intelligence or track record. It's a signal-to-noise 
call for a specific context: someone tasked with building CLG at a small 
B2B SaaS with limited time.

### Christina Garnett

Christina's focus is not on how to build and operate a community. Her 
work centers on customer trust, brand-consumer relationships, and 
consumer behavior psychology. Her "trust deposits vs trust withdrawals" 
framework is interesting as a mental model, but too conceptual if the 
reader is looking for a playbook they can apply next Monday.

Her background reinforces this. She was Principal Marketing Manager for 
Offline Community & Advocacy at HubSpot, not someone running a 4K+ 
member community like Mike Rizzo or a 3M+ member program like Erica 
Kuhl. Her skill set complements a community operator. It does not 
replace one.

I used a simple test: if I removed Christina from my ten-source list, 
would my playbook conclusions change? The answer is no. My tensions, 
my rejected ideas, and my recommendations stay valid without her. 
That's the clearest signal that she is not core to CLG understanding.

Her content is a stronger fit for people working in customer experience, 
brand marketing, or customer advocacy. It's not the right starting 
point for a junior growth marketer building CLG from zero.

### Mac Reddin

Mac is the CEO of Commsor, a platform that sells CLG infrastructure. 
That structural fact shapes almost everything he publishes. It's 
difficult to separate insights that come from real operator experience 
from framing that indirectly supports Commsor's commercial position.

The tactical parts of his work are still useful. His B2B gifting 
playbook (LEGO, hot sauce, calendars) and the "physical print test" 
for content quality are universal enough to hold up on their own. But 
his strategic framing (why companies need community infrastructure, 
why platforms matter, why offline experiences justify investment) 
consistently lands on positions that happen to align with what 
Commsor sells.

For a junior growth marketer without a filter for this bias, absorbing 
Mac's content early creates a distorted view of what CLG actually is. 
I would rather someone start with in-house operators like Mike Rizzo, 
Erica Kuhl, or Joel Primack. Once they have a foundation strong enough 
to separate operator insight from product marketing, Mac becomes 
useful supplementary reading.

### What I did NOT do here

I considered dropping Rosie Sherry because her audience skews toward 
solo community builders, not SaaS founders. I decided against it. 
Rosie's public documentation of her own workflow (pricing, member 
counts, experiments-in-public) is exactly the "practice what you 
teach" behavior I was looking for. Wrong audience fit is a weaker 
reason than domain mismatch (Christina) or commercial bias (Mac).

I also considered dropping David Spinks because his tactical playbooks 
are mostly pre-2022. I kept him because the frameworks he wrote (peak 
moments, "do things that don't scale" for community) are foundational 
enough that most other experts in this list still reference him. 
Removing him would break the reasoning chain.
